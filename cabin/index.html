    Cabin guide

    House guidelines
    • Quiet hours run 10 PM to 7 AM. Sound carries a long way over the water.
    • No smoking indoors, in the cabin or the bunkhouse.
    • Fires go in the fire pit only, and only when the fire danger is low. Drown them before bed.
    • Trash stays in the garage until it goes to the dump. Bears check the porch.
    • Kids on the dock wear life jackets.
    • Pets are welcome in the cabin and the bunkhouse. Clean up after them and be considerate.
    • Leave it better than you found it, and add anything that ran out to the grocery list.

    The lake chain

    Pokegama is one of the ten spring-fed lakes of the Lac du Flambeau chain, known for clear water and good fishing. The chain sits among more than 250 lakes around Lac du Flambeau.

    • Pokegama. About 1,050 acres, 65 feet at the deepest. Lake of the Torches casino is on its shore.
    • Fence Lake. The chain's largest lake at about 3,480 acres. Musky, walleye, northern, smallmouth and panfish.
    • Long Interlaken. About 380 acres, along downtown Lac du Flambeau.
    • Flambeau Lake. About 1,170 acres.
    • Crawling Stone and Little Crawling Stone. About 1,480 and 110 acres.

    The chain is inside the Lac du Flambeau reservation. Fishing rules for walleye and musky are in the Fishing section, and some landings on tribal land need a launch permit for non-members.

    Fishing

    In Pokegama

    • Walleye
    • Musky
    • Northern pike
    • Largemouth and smallmouth bass
    • Panfish

    1,041 acres, 65 feet at the deepest, and very clear. The bottom is mostly sand, with rock and gravel.

    Where to start

    • Walleye and musky: points, bars and the edges where the shallows drop to the basin.
    • Musky: fallen timber along the shoreline, especially with topwater.
    • Panfish: standing weeds offshore, with small jigs.

    Licenses and rules

    Bring a Wisconsin fishing license and follow Wisconsin's seasons and bag limits.

    Pokegama is one of 19 reservation lakes where the Lac du Flambeau Band voted in April 2026 to stop non-members from keeping walleye and musky. A federal court has blocked that rule while the case goes on, so for now the state rules apply. The Band asks anglers not to keep or target walleye and musky. Check for updates before keeping either one.

    Some boat landings on tribal land require a launch permit for non-members.
